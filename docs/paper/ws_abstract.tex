\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Question.} How much control over an in-plane elastic wave field can the fiber
orientation of a continuous-fiber composite exert, at fixed material and mass and under the
tow-curvature limit of automated placement? By what mechanism, and within what limits?

\noindent\textbf{Hypothesis.} In a strongly anisotropic lamina the energy of a quasi-longitudinal
(qP) wave is skewed toward the fibers, by up to $\WSkewMax^\circ$ for CFRP, so that
$\WSTwentyCFRP\%$ of wave normals carry their energy within $20^\circ$ of them. Where the field is
qP-dominated and the fibers turn slowly on the wavelength scale, a fiber field should act as a
designable field of energy streamlines, and its robust effect should be broadband
\emph{redirection} of energy.

\noindent\textbf{Analysis.} We solve the two-dimensional orthotropic time-harmonic problem in
\texttt{dolfinx} with discrete adjoints, a smooth orientation field and a pointwise tow-curvature
constraint. Every focus gain is split exactly into redirection (the target's share of the energy)
and whole-field growth, and checked against the share of injected power absorbed in the target,
a frequency sweep, the energy-flux field, a ray prediction from the fiber field alone, a series of
absorbing layers, anisotropy and curvature studies with multiple starts, and an as-built tow model.

\noindent\textbf{Results.} With an absorbing layer whose reflection is converged ($|R|\le\WReflTwo$),
an orientation-only lens redirects $\WBandConvMin$--$\WBandConvMax\times$ more energy into its target,
band-averaged over the tested wavelengths. The share of the injected power absorbed in the target
rises $\WPowPaperMin$--$\WPowPaperMax\times$ over the band (with the paper's layer), partly through resonance near the design frequency
($\WPowNormPaperMin$--$\WPowNormPaperMax\times$ as a share of the plate's structural loss). Rays traced through the fiber field predict $\WRayAbs$--$\WRayPaper\times$. The
energy flux stays within about $\WFluxDevMasked^\circ$ of the fibers wherever they turn by less than
about $4$\,rad per wavelength. The $\WGainForty\times$ single-frequency focus gain is mostly a
resonance of the finite plate (quality factor $\approx\WFineQ$). Redirection vanishes for isotropic
material, grows monotonically with anisotropy (best of four starts), and is unaffected while the
minimum tow radius is below about $0.7$ wavelengths. A full cloak reaches $\WCloakPeak\times$
far-field reduction only at its design frequency and on its design mesh ($\WCSerD\times$ on a mesh
$1.8$ times finer).

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.} Fiber orientation steers in-plane elastic energy by channeling it along
the fibers, a few-fold and broadband in the ray regime. Large single-frequency gains of finite plates
are mostly resonance and should not be reported as steering.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} elastic waves; fiber steering; anisotropic plates; energy channeling;
topology optimization; cloaking; automated fiber placement.
